Part 3 — Tips inside lessons (written in simple English)

#LessonTipCheckCorrection / comment
1Consonant SoundsArabic has /b/ but no /p/, so park can sound like bark and pin like bin . Practise /p/ with the paper test — you need a puff of air. Arabic also has no /v/ (practise very , not “fery”), and in many dialects no /tʃ/ (practise chicken , not “shicken”). Don’t add a vowel to consonant groups: street , not “istreet”; spring , not “sipring”.☐ OK
☐ Fix
2TH: think and thisGood news: Standard Arabic has both TH sounds — ث is /θ/ ( think ) and ذ is /ð/ ( this ). If your dialect uses t/d or s/z instead, use the ث and ذ you know from Standard Arabic or Qur’anic recitation.☐ OK
☐ Fix
3W vs VArabic و is just like English W, so W is easy. V does not exist in Arabic, and it often becomes /f/: “fery” for very , “fan” for van . Put your top teeth on your bottom lip and add your voice — you should feel a buzz.☐ OK
☐ Fix
4Final Consonants: bag / backArabic words rarely end in two or three consonants, so learners often add a vowel (“ask-id”) or drop a consonant. Remember: asked = “askt”, worked = “workt” — one syllable.☐ OK
☐ Fix
5Short and Long Vowels: ship / sheepArabic also has short and long vowels (كسرة and ياء), so the idea of length is familiar. But English /ɪ/ ( ship ) is lower and more relaxed than kasra, and many Arabic speakers mix up /ɪ/ and /e/: bit/bet , pin/pen , sit/set .☐ OK
☐ Fix
6cat / cut / betArabic has three main vowel qualities (a, i, u). English /æ/ ( cat ), /ʌ/ ( cut ) and /ɑ/ ( cot ) can all sound like fatḥa to you. Practise all three until you can hear the difference.☐ OK
☐ Fix
7Articles: a, an, the — or nothingArabic has a definite article ( ال ) but no indefinite article. So learners often drop a/an (“I am teacher” → I am a teacher ). Arabic also uses ال for general ideas, but English uses no article: Life is hard. I like coffee. (not “The life”, “the coffee”).☐ OK
☐ Fix
8Present: Simple vs ContinuousArabic sentences in the present often have no verb “to be”: “He teacher”, “She tired”. English always needs am / is / are : He is a teacher. She is tired.☐ OK
☐ Fix
9Present Perfect vs Past SimpleArabic uses the present tense for something that started in the past and continues: “I live here since two years.” In English: I’ve lived here for two years.☐ OK
☐ Fix
10Words That Go TogetherCommon translation traps: “open the light” → turn on the light; “close the TV” → turn off the TV; “I have 30 years” → I’m 30 ; “my brother he works” → my brother works ; “take a decision” → make a decision (in Canada).☐ OK
☐ Fix
11What Makes Canadian English CanadianIf you learned British English, remember: lift → elevator , flat → apartment , queue → line-up . Everyone will still understand the British words.☐ OK

Part 3 — Tips inside lessons (written in simple English)

#LessonTipCheckCorrection / comment
1Consonant SoundsMandarin b/p, d/t, g/k are different because of the puff of air — so you already know aspiration. But English also needs voiced sounds with a buzz: /b d g v z ʒ/. Most Mandarin syllables end in a vowel, -n or -ng, so learners drop final consonants or add a vowel (“bed-uh”). Also watch /v/ (not “w”), and /l/ vs /n/ if you are from southern China.☐ OK
☐ Fix
2TH: think and thisMandarin has no TH. Common substitutions are /s/ (“sink” for think ), /f/ (“free” for three ) and /z/ or /d/ (“zis”, “dis” for this ). Put your tongue between your teeth and blow.☐ OK
☐ Fix
3W vs VMandarin w (as in 我 wǒ ) is like English W. But Mandarin has no V, so very becomes “wery” and vest becomes “west”. For V, your top teeth must touch your bottom lip.☐ OK
☐ Fix
4Final Consonants: bag / backBecause Mandarin syllables don’t end in t, d, k, s or z, these English endings often disappear: plural -s, past -ed, possessive ’s. But they change the meaning: He work vs He worked . Say every ending clearly — even exaggerate at first.☐ OK
☐ Fix
5Short and Long Vowels: ship / sheepMandarin i (衣) is close to /iː/ ( sheep ), and u (乌) is close to /uː/ ( moon ). The short sounds /ɪ/ ( ship ) and /ʊ/ ( book ) are new — make them short and relaxed.☐ OK
☐ Fix
6cat / cut / bet/æ/ ( cat ) is new for many Mandarin speakers and often sounds like /e/ ( bad → bed ). Drop your jaw low and spread your lips.☐ OK
☐ Fix
7Rhythm: English Keeps the BeatMandarin gives every syllable a tone and about the same length. English uses pitch for the whole sentence instead, and makes small words short and weak. Try not to give every syllable equal weight.☐ OK
☐ Fix
8Articles: a, an, the — or nothingMandarin has no articles. Most common mistakes: missing a (“I have car” → I have a car ) and missing the (“Close door” → Close the door ). Use the quick test in this lesson.☐ OK
☐ Fix
9Present: Simple vs ContinuousMandarin verbs don’t change for he/she, so the -s is easy to forget: “She work” → She work s . Also, 他 and 她 sound the same ( tā ), so many speakers mix up he and she when talking. Check your pronouns!☐ OK
☐ Fix
10Present Perfect vs Past SimpleMandarin shows time with words like 了, 过 or 昨天, not by changing the verb. In English the verb must change: I went yesterday (not “I go yesterday”). For experience, use 过 → Have you ever been…?☐ OK
☐ Fix
11Words That Go TogetherCommon translation traps: “open/close the light” (开灯/关灯) → turn on/off the light; “see a book” (看书) → read a book; “eat medicine” (吃药) → take medicine; “play computer” → use the computer / play computer games; “very like” → really like .☐ OK

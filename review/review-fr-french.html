#LessonTipCheckCorrection / comment
1Consonant SoundsFrench /p t k/ have no puff of air, so Canadians may hear pin as bin . French has no H sound (see the H lesson) and no TH (see the TH lesson). Good news: French already has /ʒ/ ( je ) and /ʃ/ ( chat ).☐ OK
☐ Fix
2TH: think and thisFrench speakers often say “ze” for the and “sink” for think . Let your tongue come out between your teeth — you should be able to see it in a mirror.☐ OK
☐ Fix
3W vs VFrench has both /w/ ( oui ) and /v/ ( vous ), so this should be easy. Just remember: the letter w in English is always /w/ — never /v/ as in some French borrowed words.☐ OK
☐ Fix
4English H and RFrench has no H sound, so learners drop it (“’ello”, “’ouse”) — or add it where it doesn’t belong. Practise with a sigh: “haaa”. Remember the silent H in hour, honest, honour .☐ OK
☐ Fix
5Final Consonants: bag / backIn French, final consonants are often silent. In English, they must be pronounced: cats , liked (“likt”), helped (“helpt”).☐ OK
☐ Fix
6Short and Long Vowels: ship / sheepFrench i ( vite ) is tense, like /iː/. English /ɪ/ does not exist in French, so ship and sheep can sound the same. Relax your mouth and make /ɪ/ short.☐ OK
☐ Fix
7cat / cut / bet/æ/ ( cat ) and /ʌ/ ( cut ) are new. /æ/ often comes out like French a or è . Open your jaw more.☐ OK
☐ Fix
8Word StressFrench puts the stress at the end of a word group, so learners often stress the last syllable of English words: “compu TER ”, “deve LOP ”. English stress can be anywhere — learn it with each word: com PU ter, de VEL op.☐ OK
☐ Fix
9Rhythm: English Keeps the BeatFrench is syllable-timed — every syllable about the same length. English is stress-timed: strong syllables are long, small words are very short.☐ OK
☐ Fix
10Articles: a, an, the — or nothingFrench uses an article for general ideas; English doesn’t: J’aime le café → I like coffee ; La vie est belle → Life is beautiful .☐ OK
☐ Fix
11Present: Simple vs ContinuousThe French present covers both I work and I’m working . In English, choose: habit → I work ; right now → I’m working .☐ OK
☐ Fix
12Present Perfect vs Past SimpleFrench depuis + present → English present perfect: J’habite ici depuis deux ans = I’ve lived here for two years (not “I live here since two years”).☐ OK
